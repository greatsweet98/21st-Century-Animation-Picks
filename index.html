The history of animation is extensive and intriguing, dating back over a century to the first days of motion pictures. Animation techniques have changed dramatically from the first hand-drawn cartoons to the most recent computer-generated imagery (CGI), from basic flickering pictures to a powerful cultural impact in entertainment and other fields. Animation has challenged the limits of artistic expression and narrative while providing viewers with entertainment over the years. From Mickey Mouse to Homer Simpson, it has produced some of the most recognizable figures in popular culture and opened the door for innovative cinema and television approaches. The discipline of animation was completely transformed with the introduction of photography and film in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, which gave rise to new methods and instruments for producing and preserving motion. Cel animation and stop-motion animation were two of the most important animation styles to develop during this period. In stop-motion animation, a succession of static pictures is created, with slight adjustments made in between, and then they are played back sequentially to give the impression of motion. The development of the camera, which enabled animators to record and duplicate pictures more quickly and precisely than ever before, made the method conceivable. Early animated films, such "The Enchanted Drawing" by J. Stuart Blackton from 1900, made heavy use of stop-motion animation. To give the appearance of motion, cel animation, also referred to as conventional animation, entails hand-drawing each frame of an animated sequence and taking successive photos of them. The development of celluloid sheets, which could be rapidly sketched and photographed, allowed for the use of this technology. Cel animation was widely utilized in movies and rose to prominence in the early 20th century (Abitbul, 2025).

The 1930s to 1960s marked the start of animation's golden period. Because of the development of sound, color, and multiplane cameras at this time, it was dubbed the "golden age of animation." Steamboat Willie, a short animation, is thought to have marked the start of the golden era in 1928. This is due to the fact that it was the first time sound and animation were linked, making the experience more immersive. Walt Disney is the one who produced this iconic moment in history. Walt Disney is credited with being the forerunner of animation and cartoons throughout the golden era, and his company continues to have a significant influence on modern society. Disney is in charge of producing well-known animated series and characters, such as Mickey Mouse. Disney is frequently given a lot of the credit, but he wasn't alone. Ub Iwerks, a longtime friend and collaborator, had a big impact on his work since he gave Disney the first illustrations for characters like Mickey Mouse. Disney was also in charge of developing the multiplane camera in 1937, which gave animation a new feeling of depth and realism and fundamentally altered the animation industry. Disney went on to make a number of other wildly successful animated animations, such as Bambi in 1942, Dumbo in 194, and Pinocchio in 1940. For many years, this fashion was the most fashionable, but in the late 1960s, it began to gradually lose favor as new technology brought about yet another revolution. As the golden period came to an end, a variety of methods and styles emerged that eventually came to define modern animation. The popularity of television, which by 1964 was believed to be in 92% of American households, was the main cause of this quick transition. Animations were entirely hand-drawn prior to 1960. Early computers, however, revolutionized the animation business by providing a completely new method of creation. The first computer-generated animation was Hummingbird, which debuted in 1967. The work was made by animators using 30,000 pictures, and while it may appear unnecessary now, it revolutionized animation. A lot of individuals started to like viewing cartoons at home. Studios were forced to adjust and begin producing animated cartoons for television as a result, and by the middle of the 1980s, cartoons were widely used. Advances in technology make it possible to produce animation more rapidly, with greater detail, and with longer films. CGI has been used to generate several animated feature films since the 1980s. The term "computer-generated image" refers to the process of creating 3D models of characters or environments using a laptop instead of by hand. Even with all of the amazing progress that animation has made thus far, it continues to improve year. Animation creators are constantly trying to figure out how to employ the newest technology, and new developments like virtual reality are probably going to have a big influence on the field. Animation is now utilized in a wide range of media, including video games, marketing, education, and much more. It is no longer limited to cinema and television (Abitbul, 2025).

As animation developed, it got more popular and some of the features that were frequent in earlier episodes became less common. We began to see more stories, where people evolved and interacted with a narrative, in place of the one-off programs where each day is a new day. There was more fantasy and world-building than the usual squash and stretch folks in commonplace settings. The portrayal of women protagonists, African Americans, and even the LGBTQ+ group has to be changed. Amphibia, Craig of the Creek, Kipo, and The Hollow are a few outstanding instances of them. It proves that individuals are capable of positive development and increased acceptance. Additionally, cartoons are starting to incorporate many ethnicities and beliefs; Victor and Valentino are a prime example of this. "Animation is an amazing medium that can connect with audiences of many demographics not only because it makes complex concepts easier to digest, but doing so in a visually captivating way," said Rachel Immel, a former Crimsonian writer and editor who now works in the field of animation. Even though a lot of these shows are "aimed towards kids," animation is currently addressing a wide range of subjects in various ways, including diversity, love, grief, mental health, and many more. As their characters confront situations and obstacles that are familiar to the human experience, cartoonists and storytellers are able to construct a stylized world from the ground up, helping to captivate their audience. Over the years, there have also been significant changes in child safety. Characters who were nasty, malevolent, or even tough-looking were frequently shown smoking or drinking cold beverages during the heyday of animation. That wouldn't fly at all nowadays. Large corporations always strive to ensure that the animated cartoons are completely suitable for children, even going so far as to label them as "too kid-friendly."
